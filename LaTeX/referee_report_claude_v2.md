# Referee report — second round

**Manuscript:** "Neutrino masses and mixing angles in the Plan B Model"
**Authors:** B. Allanach, P. C. Sarjapur

## General assessment

I thank the authors for a substantial and constructive revision. Many of the points from my first report have been dealt with properly: the sign of the $\epsilon^2$ correction in Eq. (2.13) is now correct; the self-contradictory paragraph in the inverted-hierarchy (IH) section has been removed and replaced by an honest statement that $\epsilon\sim0.03$ cannot reproduce the angles; the appendix spurion charges have been repaired and are now internally consistent, with a welcome new table of field content from which invariance of (A.5)–(A.6) can be verified by inspection, and the spurion VEV insertions fix the earlier dimensional problem; the Bayesian evidences and the NH/IH Bayes factor are now quoted; convergence criteria, package versions, the asymmetric-error treatment and a tagged GitHub release have been added; the cosmology/$0\nu\beta\beta$ paragraph is now referenced; and the manuscript hygiene is much improved.

However, the revision has introduced a new technical error in the IH mixing-matrix analysis (point 1 below, which I have checked numerically), and three points from my first report remain unaddressed in substance. I also note two remaining referee-visible author notes. I recommend **minor-to-moderate revision**; none of the remaining items should require new large-scale computation, but point 1 does require re-derivation of one matrix.

## Remaining and new points

**1. (New) The rewritten IH PMNS matrix has incorrect $\epsilon$-scalings.**
The revised $U$ below Eq. (2.31) asserts $U_{\mu3}=-\epsilon u^{(1)}_{13}+\epsilon^2 u^{(2)}_{13}$, $U_{\tau1}=2\epsilon u^{(1)}_{13}$ and $U_{\tau2}=\epsilon^2 u^{(2)}_{32}$. Direct eigenvector analysis of the texture contradicts all three. Writing the lightest eigenvector as $(\delta_1,\delta_2,1)$ and solving $(m_\nu-\lambda_3)v=0$ order by order gives $\delta_1=U_{e3}\sim\epsilon$ but $\delta_2=U_{\mu3}\sim\epsilon^{2}$ (the $\mu$-component is sourced only through $\tilde X^{(1)}_{23}$ against the $\epsilon^{-3}$ leading block); and the heavy pseudo-Dirac pair has $\tau$-components $U_{\tau1},U_{\tau2}$ *both* of order $\epsilon$, with opposite signs. I have confirmed this numerically with random $\mathcal{O}(1)$ couplings and the texture $a_{11}=a_{13}=0$: over $\epsilon\in[0.003,0.1]$ the medians scale as $|U_{e3}|\approx1.0\,\epsilon$, $|U_{\mu3}|\approx1.7\,\epsilon^{2}$, $|U_{\tau1}|\approx0.65\,\epsilon$, $|U_{\tau2}|\approx0.72\,\epsilon$ — and $|U_{\tau1}|/|U_{e3}|\to0.66$, not the factor 2 the printed matrix implies. The final expression for $\tan\theta_{23}$ also contains a coefficient $u^{(1)}_{23}$ that does not appear anywhere in the displayed matrix.

Two consequences. First, the matrix and angle formulae must be re-derived (I would encourage doing so symbolically with the same code used for the repository, and validating against exact numerical diagonalisation — this would have caught the error). Secondly, and more physically: since $\tan\theta_{23}\sim\epsilon^{2}$, matching the near-maximal atmospheric angle requires the relevant coefficient to be $\approx1.1/\epsilon^{2}\approx8$ at the posterior $\epsilon\approx0.36$. This is precisely analogous to the $u^{(2)}_{13}\in[4.8,6.5]$ enhancement in the NH case, and should be stated with the same candour.

**2. (Carried over) Validity of the $\epsilon$-expansion where the fit lives, and what the spread $r$ does and does not measure.**
My first-report request here remains unmet. In both hierarchies the phenomenologically successful region is one where a nominally subleading term is comparable to the leading one ($\epsilon^2u^{(2)}_{13}\approx0.85$–$1.15$ in NH; the $\mathcal{O}(8)$ coefficient in IH per point 1). In that regime the truncation at $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$ is not obviously controlled, and the reader cannot tell whether the quoted analytic formulae describe the posterior region even qualitatively. The fix is cheap: take a handful of posterior samples, compare the analytic masses/angles against exact diagonalisation, and either report agreement or soften the analytic claims to order-of-magnitude statements. Separately, the sentence "We defer the rigorous proof of this naturalness to the subsequent global Bayesian scan" should be revised: the scan's $r$-statistic is built from the fundamental $y_{ij}$ only and is insensitive to the $u^{(2)}_{13}$ (and now $\theta_{23}$) enhancements, so it cannot prove the claim as stated. Showing the posterior distribution of $u^{(2)}_{13}$ (or simply of $X^{(0)}_{11}$'s coefficient), or the prior fraction achieving $\sin\theta_{13}\le0.15$, would do the job honestly and would, I suspect, still support the authors' qualitative conclusion.

**3. (Carried over) The required $B/A$ suppression in the IH case is still unquantified.**
The text now correctly says the coefficients "$A/B$ need to be larger", but does not say by how much: Eq. (2.31) at the posterior $\epsilon\approx0.36$ requires $B/A\approx0.02$, a factor of $\sim50$ — outside the paper's own $r\le30$ comfort zone if it were a ratio of fundamental couplings. Since $A$ and $B$ are rational functions of the $a_{ij},y_{ij}$, the heavy-tail argument may well cover this, but the number should be stated and the point addressed explicitly, as it bears directly on the paper's headline naturalness comparison between the orderings.

**4. (Carried over, still incorrect) The IH heavy-neutrino spectrum in the Conclusions.**
The revision changed $(M_0,\ \epsilon^2M_0,\ \epsilon^4M_0)$ to $(M_0,\ \epsilon^2M_0,\ \epsilon^3M_0)$, but neither is right for the IH texture. With $a_{11}=a_{13}=0$, $M$ has entries $(\epsilon^3a_{12},\ \epsilon^4a_{22},\ \epsilon^2a_{23},\ a_{33})$ only; integrating out the third state, the upper $2\times2$ block is $\begin{pmatrix}0&\epsilon^3a_{12}\\ \epsilon^3a_{12}&\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^4)\end{pmatrix}M_0$, giving a **quasi-degenerate pair** at $\sim\epsilon^3a_{12}M_0$ split at relative order $\epsilon$, i.e. spectrum $\approx(a_{33}M_0,\ \epsilon^3M_0,\ \epsilon^3M_0)$. This is worth getting right because it is interesting: a split of relative order $\epsilon$ in a heavy pseudo-Dirac pair is exactly the configuration in which resonant leptogenesis is discussed, strengthening the authors' own remark about complexifying the couplings.

**5. (Partially addressed) The $M_0$ versus $\Lambda_{\rm FN}$ scale structure.**
The new sentence stating that $M_0$ is independent of $\Lambda_{\rm FN}$, and the acknowledgement that the renormalisable $\langle\theta\rangle\overline{\nu^c_{R1}}\nu_{R3}$ operator contributes negligibly ($\sim10^{12}$ below $M_0\epsilon a_{13}$), are welcome — and to answer the query left in the margin: yes, that part of the argument is sound, and it moreover shows the renormalisable term cannot spoil the IH texture zero either (it regenerates $a_{13}^{\rm eff}\sim10^{-12}$, utterly negligible). What is still missing is the *positive* half of the argument: which dynamics generates entries of size $M_0\,\epsilon^{|X_i+X_j|}$ at all. FN mediators at $\Lambda_{\rm FN}$ alone would give $\sim\Lambda_{\rm FN}\epsilon^{n}$-scale entries, not $M_0\epsilon^n$. The appendix's $\Phi_{ij}$ spurions and Fig. 8 clearly gesture at the answer (lepton-number-violating VEVs $\langle\Phi_{ij}\rangle\sim M_0$ feeding Majorana masses through the $F_i$ at $M_0$, dressed by $\theta$/$\Lambda_{\rm FN}$ chains), but the power counting of Fig. 8 is never done: the reader should be shown that the diagram yields $M_0\epsilon^{|X_i+X_j|}$ rather than, say, $\Lambda_{\rm FN}^2/M_0\times\epsilon^n$. One explicit paragraph (or a footnote with the operator dimension count) in §2.2, cross-referencing the appendix, would close this. While editing that sentence, please also correct "independant", "Frogatt-Nielson", and the italic $\Lambda_{FN}$.

**6. (Carried over) "Percent-level" is still inconsistent with the paper's own numbers.**
Introduction and Conclusions still say the NH requires suppression of one coupling "at the percent level", while §2.2 derives $y_{22}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)\approx0.16$ and the appendix example delivers $q^N=0.13$. These are the ten-to-twenty percent level. Please reconcile (this affects the abstract's framing too: for the IH the suppressed couplings are Majorana-sector $a_{ij}$, though I note the Conclusions now correctly say "dimensionless couplings").

**7. Bayes factor: a caveat is needed.**
Quoting $\ln Z_{\rm NH}=-28.03\pm0.02$, $\ln Z_{\rm IH}=-29.07\pm0.03$ and their ratio is a good addition. However, the two evidences are computed against *different* likelihood targets (the NO and IO columns of NuFIT, which are separate conditional fits), and the NuFIT $\Delta\chi^2$ between orderings — genuine data information — is not included. Strictly, a Bayes factor requires a common dataset. I do not ask for a re-analysis, but a sentence acknowledging this limitation (and that the quoted 2.8 is therefore indicative only) is necessary; "anecdotal" could usefully cite the Jeffreys/Kass–Raftery scale being invoked. Also "in favor" → "in favour" given the paper's British orthography.

**8. Remaining author notes and small errors.**
Two marginal notes remain visible: "is this argument airtight?" in §2.2 (see point 5 for the answer) and "is this second reference required?" in the Conclusions — on the latter: I would keep it, since the companion reference documents that the DESI bound relaxes under PR4 likelihoods/supernovae, which is precisely the fairness the sentence needs; consider adding the DESI DR2 result as well. The `\bca`/`\pcs` macros should be deleted. In addition: (i) Table 5 lists $\nu_{1,2,3}$ as "LH Fermion" — in the body of the paper they are right-handed fields; (ii) the sentence "have a negative unit charge $(-1,0,0),(0,-1,0)$ and $(0,0,-1)$..." should be recast (the charges are not all "negative unit"); (iii) $U(1)_{B_3-L_\mu}$ in the Conclusions should be $U(1)_{B_3-3L_\mu}$ (the title of the cited paper); (iv) "NuFit 6.0" vs "NuFIT 6.0"; (v) Eq. (2.20) still ends in a comma followed by a new sentence; (vi) the normal-ordering counterparts of the cosmology predictions ($\Sigma m_\nu\approx0.06$ eV, $m_{\beta\beta}\sim1$–4 meV) are still absent and would take one sentence — the contrast between the orderings' testability is a selling point, not a liability.

## Recommendation

The paper is close. Points 1 and 4 are objective errors that must be corrected; points 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7 require honest sentences and small computations rather than new research; point 8 is cosmetic. I expect to be able to recommend publication once these are dealt with, and I do not anticipate needing to see the manuscript again if the authors' response addresses point 1 with a validated re-derivation.
