# Referee report

**Manuscript:** "Neutrino masses and mixing angles in the Plan B Model"
**Authors:** B. Allanach, P. C. Sarjapur
**Journal:** JHEP

## Summary

The authors study the neutrino sector of the "Plan B Model", a $U(1)_{3B_3-L_1-2L_2}$ extension of the Standard Model previously proposed to ameliorate the neutral-current $B$-anomalies. The three right-handed neutrinos required by anomaly cancellation permit a type-I seesaw, and the family-dependent $U(1)_X$ charges, broken by a TeV-scale flavon, generate Froggatt–Nielsen (FN) textures for the neutrino Dirac and Majorana matrices. The authors show analytically that the unadorned texture fails to reproduce the PMNS matrix, and that one additional suppression — $y_{22}\to0$ for normal ordering, or $a_{11},a_{13}\to0$ for inverted ordering — repairs it. They perform nested-sampling fits to the NuFIT 6.0 observables for both orderings, quantify naturalness via the coupling spread $r$, and present a clockwork mechanism in an appendix that could furnish the required texture zeros.

The subject is timely and appropriate for JHEP: connecting a phenomenologically successful $Z'$ model of the $b\to s\ell\ell$ data to the neutrino sector is a natural and worthwhile exercise, the use of a quantified naturalness measure (comparing posterior to prior) is a welcome methodological point, and the conclusion that the *inverted* ordering is the more economical outcome is interesting given that cosmology is beginning to disfavour it. I can envisage recommending publication, but not in the manuscript's current state. There are several places where the physics argument is internally inconsistent or overstated, one equation that I believe is simply wrong, and — I must be blunt — the manuscript has clearly been submitted with editing debris still in place (authors' notes to one another appear in a table caption, a figure caption and twice in the body text). I request major revisions.

## Major points

**1. Validity of the $\epsilon$-expansion in the normal-hierarchy angle analysis (§2.3).**
The revised analysis honestly concedes that $\sin\theta_{13}=|U_{e3}|\approx|1-\epsilon^2 u^{(2)}_{13}|$, so that matching the reactor angle requires $u^{(2)}_{13}\in[4.8,6.5]$, i.e. $\epsilon^2 u^{(2)}_{13}\approx 1$. But if the "$\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$ correction" cancels the leading term to 15%, the expansion is no longer under perturbative control at that point in parameter space: nothing guarantees that the neglected $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^3)$ terms, whose coefficients may be comparably enhanced, are small. The same concern applies to $\tan\theta_{23}=u^{(1)}_{23}/u^{(1)}_{33}$, a ratio of subleading coefficients of the very column whose leading entry has been cancelled, and to the neglected normalisation of that column (which is *not* close to unity precisely when the cancellation operates). The authors should (i) validate the expansion against exact numerical diagonalisation in the posterior region — they possess the machinery to do this trivially — and either demonstrate its adequacy or present §2.3's angle formulae as qualitative only; and (ii) note that the spread statistic $r$, computed from the fundamental $y_{ij}$ alone, does not quantify the $\theta_{13}$ cancellation at all. A direct measure of how much of the prior volume achieves $\sin\theta_{13}\le0.15$ (or the posterior distribution of $u^{(2)}_{13}$, or of $X^{(0)}_{11}$) would substantiate the claim that "such cancellation routinely occurs". As it stands, "We defer the rigorous proof of this naturalness to the subsequent global Bayesian scan" defers to a scan that measures something else.

**2. Self-contradiction in the inverted-hierarchy analysis (§2.4).**
Above Eq. (2.31) the authors correctly state that $\epsilon\sim0.03$ "would fail to reproduce the mixing angles", so that $A/B$ must be large. Immediately below the angle formulae, however, the old text survives: "We have therefore shown again that if the $u^{(k)}_{ij}$ parameters are of order 1 and $\epsilon\sim0.03$, then we can fit these approximations to the data." These two statements cannot both stand; with $\epsilon\sim0.03$ one finds $\tan\theta_{23}\approx\epsilon u^{(1)}_{23}\lesssim0.1$ against a measured $\approx1.1$. Please rewrite the paragraph coherently around the actual fit value ($\epsilon\approx0.36$, per the commented-out posterior). Moreover, at $\epsilon\approx0.36$, Eq. (2.31) requires $B/A\approx0.02$ — a suppression of order fifty in a ratio of $\mathcal{O}(1)$-built quantities. Since the paper's naturalness criterion is $r\le30$ for the fundamental couplings, the authors should state explicitly how large a spread this induced $B/A$ suppression corresponds to, or explain why (as a ratio of rational functions with heavy-tailed distributions) it should not be counted as tuning. The reader is currently left to guess.

**3. Sign error in Eq. (2.13) ($m_{\nu_3}$).**
Second-order perturbation theory gives $\lambda = \lambda_0 + \epsilon^2\left[X^{(2)}_{22} + (X^{(1)}_{12})^2/\lambda_0\right]$: the level-repulsion term necessarily pushes the large eigenvalue *away* from the light ones, so the $\epsilon^2$ bracket should enter with a **plus** sign (the $(y_{12}\alpha_{22}+y_{11}\alpha_{12})^2/\alpha_{22}$ term carries the sign of $\lambda_0$, and hence always increases the magnitude). I have checked this against exact diagonalisation at small $\epsilon$: the plus-sign expression reproduces the exact eigenvalue to $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^4)$ accuracy; the printed minus-sign version does not. Please correct, and confirm the corresponding expressions in the repository/numerics are unaffected.

**4. Effective-field-theory consistency of the Majorana texture, Eq. (2.7).**
The Majorana matrix is written as $M_{ij}=M_0\,a_{ij}\,(\langle\theta\rangle/\Lambda_{\rm FN})^{|X_i+X_j|}$ with $M_0\sim10^{13\text{--}17}$ GeV while $\langle\theta\rangle\sim$ TeV and $\Lambda_{\rm FN}$ is a few TeV. Two questions require an answer in the text. First, $\theta\,\overline{\nu^c_{R1}}\nu_{R3}$ is gauge invariant and *renormalisable*; with an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ coefficient it contributes $M_{13}\sim\langle\theta\rangle\sim$ TeV, which is what the effective theory generically provides — not $M_0\,\epsilon$. Second, FN mediators at $\Lambda_{\rm FN}$ generate $M_{ij}\sim\Lambda_{\rm FN}\,\epsilon^{|X_i+X_j|}$, not $M_0\,\epsilon^{|X_i+X_j|}$; obtaining the latter presupposes lepton-number-violating dynamics near $M_0$, in which case the expansion parameter for those operators is not obviously the same $\epsilon$. The construction may well be defensible (e.g. via heavy SM-singlet fields near $M_0$ whose couplings to $\theta$ generate the texture, along the lines gestured at in Fig. 8), but at present the paper's central texture is asserted rather than derived, and the renormalisable $\theta\overline{\nu^c_{R1}}\nu_{R3}$ term appears to be silently set to zero. A paragraph justifying Eq. (2.7) is essential.

**5. The heavy-neutrino spectrum quoted in the Conclusions is that of the wrong texture.**
The Conclusions state that "In the inverted ordering case, the heavy neutrino mass spectrum (approximately $M_0,\ \epsilon^2M_0,\ \epsilon^4M_0$) is of interest." That spectrum follows from the *generic* Majorana texture (and hence applies to the normal-hierarchy case, where the full $M$ of Eq. (2.8) is retained). With the inverted-ordering texture zeros $a_{11}=a_{13}=0$, the upper $2\times2$ block is dominated by the off-diagonal $\epsilon^3 a_{12}$ entry, giving a quasi-degenerate (pseudo-Dirac) heavy pair at $\sim\epsilon^3M_0$ alongside $a_{33}M_0$. Please correct; the distinction is physically relevant (resonant leptogenesis with a quasi-degenerate heavy pair is a natural remark here).

**6. Appendix A: the spurion charge assignments do not appear internally consistent, and Eq. (A.5) is dimensionally incomplete.**
With $\nu_{1,2,3}$ (and $F_{1,2,3}$) each carrying charge $-1$ under $U(1)_{A,B,C}$ respectively, the Majorana bilinear $\overline{\nu^c_i}\nu_j$ carries charge $-(\hat e_i+\hat e_j)$, whose entries are all of the *same* sign. No single convention (coupling via $S$ or $S^\dagger$) then permits the mixed-sign spurion $(-1,+1,0)$ to compensate the $a_{12}$ bilinear, while simultaneously $(0,-2,0)$ compensates $a_{22}$. Either the charges are misprinted or the transformation properties of the chain fields absorb the difference — in which case they must be stated. Relatedly: in Eq. (A.5) the terms $a_0\overline{\psi_{L0}}F_2^c$ and $a_1\overline{\psi_{R1}}F_2$ are dimension-three operators with apparently dimensionless coefficients (contrast the $y_L\mu$ factor in Eq. (A.1)); a mass scale or spurion VEV is missing. Note also that Eq. (A.5) uses a Majorana-type link $\mu q\,\overline{\psi^c_{R1}}\psi_{R0}$ where Eq. (A.6) uses a Dirac-type $\mu q\,\overline{\chi_{L0}}\chi_{R1}$ — if the asymmetry is intended, explain it. Since the appendix is the paper's only concrete mechanism for the texture zeros on which everything rests, it deserves to be watertight: I recommend a small table of the appendix's field content with all charges, from which invariance of (A.5)–(A.6) can be checked by inspection.

**7. "Percent-level" suppression is inconsistent with the paper's own numbers.**
The Introduction and Conclusions repeatedly say the normal hierarchy needs a coupling suppressed "at the percent level". Section 2.2 requires $y_{22}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)\approx0.16$, and the clockwork example delivers $0.13$ — the *twenty*-percent level. One of these must yield. (If the authors intend $y_{22}^2\sim0.02$, say so, but the fundamental coupling is $y_{22}$, and the spread argument at the end of §2.2 — "a modest spread of at least 6" — plainly treats the suppression as $\approx0.16$.)

**8. Manuscript hygiene.**
The submission contains what are evidently authors' working notes: the Table 2 caption ends "BCA: How did we deal with the asymmetric errors case? PCS: mean of the upper and lower bounds"; "\pcs{change here}" appears twice in the body text (end of §2.3's $\theta_{13}$ paragraph, and after Eq. (2.31)); and the caption of Fig. 7 contains the sentence "$\Delta m_{el}^2=\Delta m_{32}^2=-2.484\times10^{-3}$ is listed with the wrong sign and no units here" — a note about an error in the *plot itself*, which must instead be fixed in the figure. The figure axes should also be checked for legibility (the corner-plot labels are small) and the dashed lines in the 1D panels explained. This must all be cleaned up before the paper can be considered further; the asymmetric-error symmetrisation should be stated properly in the text.

**9. Missing citations in the new cosmology/$0\nu\beta\beta$ paragraph.**
The Conclusions now (rightly) note that the inverted ordering predicts $\Sigma m_\nu\approx0.10$ eV, "in tension with the more aggressive DESI+CMB combinations", and $m_{\beta\beta}=18$–$48$ meV "within reach of LEGEND-1000/nEXO" — but not one of these claims carries a reference. Please cite the DESI BAO analyses (and, in fairness, the analyses showing the bound relaxes with PR4 likelihoods/supernovae), the current KamLAND-Zen limit, and the LEGEND-1000 and nEXO projections. The corresponding normal-ordering predictions ($\Sigma m_\nu\approx0.06$ eV, $m_{\beta\beta}\sim1$–$4$ meV) should be given for contrast, since the near-massless lightest neutrino is a genuine prediction of the model in both orderings.

## Minor points

1. Eq. (1.4): the right-hand column vector should be $({\bm\nu}_L^c,\ {\bm\nu}_R)^T$ — the second entry is still barred; and the stray "$,\ +H.c.,$" punctuation should be repaired.
2. Eq. (1.6): "$\label{mnu_eff}.$" produces a doubled full stop.
3. Table 2: stray backtick after $33.68^{+0.73}_{-0.70}$; column heads read "Normal Hierarchy"/"Inverted Ordering" (choose a consistent pair, likewise "normal hierarchy corresponds to..." mid-caption should start upper-case); the table mixes booktabs rules with `\hline` and vertical rules.
4. Terminology drifts throughout between "normal hierarchy"/"normal ordering" and "Type I"/"Type-I"/"type-I"; standardise.
5. Eq. (2.20): $\Delta m^2_{21}\propto B_2^2-B_1^2$, not $(B_2-B_1)^2$; as an order-of-magnitude statement it survives, but the equation should be right. The sentence following it begins "The PMNS matrix..." after a comma.
6. Eq. (2.16)/(2.21): $Y_{\nu L}$ persists in Eq. (2.16) though the matrix is called $Y_\nu$ at Eq. (2.8); $m^{IO}$ is defined with a superscript but used as $m_{IO}$ in the following equations; $m_{NH}$/$m_{IO}$ notation should match.
7. §2.4, first paragraph: the parenthetical crediting Ref. [Frampton et al.] reads awkwardly ("acceptable with the dataset up until 2002") and might better note that the present $M$-texture zeros are in the *Majorana* matrix of the heavy sector rather than $m_\nu$ itself, so the correspondence with that reference's classification is indirect.
8. Appendix, opening sentence after Eq. (A.1) region: "...whose heavy mass eigenstates we integrate out such that the kinetic terms can be neglected.\ for our treatment, coupling to..." — broken sentence from editing.
9. "We note that in Ref. [Alonso et al.]..." — fine, but earlier (§ Conclusions) $U(1)_{B_3-L_\mu}$ misnames Bonilla et al.'s symmetry, which is $B_3-3L_\mu$.
10. The abstract and Conclusions describe the inverted-ordering suppressions as "Yukawa couplings"; $a_{11},a_{13}$ are Majorana-sector couplings. "Fundamental dimensionless couplings" would be accurate everywhere.
11. Figures are included from `../Python/Data/...`; for arXiv/JHEP the figure files must ship with the source.
12. "naturalness ratio CDF of $P(r<30)=68\pm2\%$" — the quantity is a probability, not a CDF value at fixed argument... actually it is the CDF at 30; the phrase "exhibits a naturalness ratio CDF of" is garbled nonetheless. Also "$N_{\rm live}=50\ 000$" vs "$50\,000$" typography.
13. Last paragraph of §2.4: "some mild factors are required between the dimensionless couplings" — presumably "mild hierarchies among".
14. The likelihood is described only via one sentence in the Table 2 caption. A sentence in the text of §2.3 stating exactly which five observables enter, with symmetrised Gaussian errors, and how the mass ordering is imposed on samples, would aid reproducibility; quoting the Bayesian evidences $\ln Z$ for the two orderings (available from UltraNest at no extra cost) would let the reader compare NO and IO directly and would strengthen the paper's headline message.
15. The `\bca`/`\pcs` macro definitions should be deleted along with their uses.

## Recommendation

The physics case is interesting and much of the numerical work appears sound; the prior-versus-posterior naturalness comparison is a good feature. However, given the internal contradictions (major points 1, 2, 7), the incorrect equation (3) and conclusion statement (5), the unjustified central texture (4), the apparently inconsistent appendix construction (6), and the state of the manuscript (8), I recommend **major revision**. I would be happy to review a revised version.
